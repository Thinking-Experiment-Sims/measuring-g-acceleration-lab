Measuring “g” on Earth’s Surface: Photogates & Ticker Tape Lab

Determine the acceleration due to gravity on Earth by comparing two classic experimental methods: 4 dual-beam digital photogates and a 60 Hz ticker tape timer with quadratic curve fitting.

Standard: g = 9.80 m/s²

1. Laboratory Setup & Pedagogical Guide

Learn the principles of dual-sensor photogates and 60 Hz ticker timers, identify equipment components, and understand how data is collected.

🔬 Laboratory Equipment & Apparatus Orientation

Dual-Sensor Photogate Principles Each modern digital photogate unit contains two internal optical sensors (Gate 1 at the top and Gate 2 at the bottom, spaced exactly 2.0 cm apart). Across 4 photogates, students collect 8 distinct position and time data points!

Experimental Protocol

  1. Measure Gate Heights: Always align your eye level with the small optical hole of each sensor. Measure both Gate 1 and Gate 2.
  2. Record Block Times: When the falling cylinder cuts through the light beam, the digital sensor state transitions from 0 to 1. Record this exact timestamp.
  3. Watch for Sensor Shuffling: In digital setups, wireless sensors can occasionally report channels out of physical vertical order. Always match the channel ID to its measured height!
Measurement Best Practices
  • Height Measurement: Align the ruler's horizontal sightline directly with the sensor beam aperture before recording each height.
  • Release Technique: Support the cylinder gently right above the first gate and release smoothly from rest.
  • Data Verification: Compare the recorded timestamps with the physical gate order on the stand.
The 60 Hz Spark / Ticker Mechanism An AC electromagnetic clapper vibrates up and down at 60 cycles per second (60 Hz). Every strike on the rotating carbon disc transfers a dot to the moving paper tape. The time interval between every dot is:
Δt = 1 / 60 s ≈ 0.01667 s

Measuring Protocol

  1. Tape Alignment: Thread the paper strip under the carbon disc so the striker pin makes clear carbon impressions.
  2. Choose Dot 0 (Origin): The first marks made while holding the tape at rest are crowded. Select the first clearly distinct dot and label it Dot 0 (where t = 0 s, y = 0 m).
  3. Measure 12 Consecutive Dots: Using the calibrated metric ruler, measure positions for 12 consecutive points (Dot 0 to Dot 11) relative to Dot 0. Do not skip dots!
Measurement Best Practices Ensure the ruler's 0 cm line is aligned exactly with Dot 0. Use the vertical sightline hairline to read every dot's position to the nearest millimeter before recording.

Teacher Grading Rubric & Accepted Explanations
  • Valid Reason 1 - Mechanical Friction (Ticker Tape): Physical drag between the striker pin, carbon disc, and tape guide slots reduces net acceleration below 9.80 m/s².
  • Valid Reason 2 - Human Hand Release Jitter (Photogates): If the object is released with an upward/downward nudge (v0y ≠ 0) or tilts while cutting the beam, the effective path timing is altered.
  • Valid Reason 3 - Parallax & Measuring Scale: Sighting the small photogate beam holes against a meter stick introduces ±1–2 mm parallax uncertainty.
  • Question 4 Rubric - Linearization Assumptions: The linear model y = M·(t²) + K assumes v₀ = 0 because it contains only a constant and a t² term with no linear t term. When the timer begins at Gate 1 after falling, v₀ ≠ 0; the missing v₀·t term bends the y vs. t² points, lowering R² and skewing the slope.
  • Invalid Student Explanations: "Human reaction time" (invalid because sensors and timers record automatically), or "gravity changes day to day".
